## The monologue

> Come in. Mind the third step. It bites. I am Marlok, Keeper of the Ember Tithe. For forty years I have taken one coin in seven from every hearth in the Marches. The ledger knows your name. It knows the goat you hid—no. Not the goat. The point is the tithe. The point is always the tithe. My uncle wore this crown before me. My cousin wore it after him. Both of them—well. The crown fits. The chair does not. Sit. The tea is cold. And when the seventh coin falls, remember: I counted it twice, and I counted it wrong.

Marlok is a specific villain — name, title, a forty-year tithe scheme, a
crown passed down from his uncle — and his tiredness is never announced:
it lives in the rhythm, the goat deflection, the trail-offs, the tithe
loop. The sting lands in the last line: the feared tithe-keeper counted
the seventh coin twice, and counted it wrong. 101 words, 516 characters,
520 bytes — inside the 1–600 gate and short enough to help on the byte
tie-break.

## How it was chosen

Three original variants were written fresh for this bounty (Marlok the
tithe-keeper, Vesna of the glass river, Balthazar the Rat-King) and judged
blind, pairwise, in both orders, against the published judge brief
(on-subject, tiredness felt not announced, specific villain, speakable
sting, near 100 words). Marlok won 2–0 against both siblings (margins 2
and 2) and 2–0 against a generic-dark-lord control (margin 4 in both
orders). See `variants.md` and `variants-self-judge.md` in the work
directory for the full scoring.

## Run

```sh
python3 main.py
```

Writes the monologue to `/output/entry.txt` (UTF-8, 101 words, 516
characters, inside the 1–600 gate). Pure Python standard library only;
the output is deterministic — the same bytes on every run.
